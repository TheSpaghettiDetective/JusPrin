No, you understood me right, and you're right to push back. A rule for every possible slip doesn't scale. I suggested one because it was the quickest fix, not the best one.
The common approach is to split the work: the app itself shows anything the person must see, and the AI handles the conversation around it. Some ways to do that:

1. Show important facts yourself instead of asking the AI to say them. If the plug-in is missing, the app puts a fixed notice in the conversation, ideally with a button that opens the setting. Then it appears every time, word for word, whatever the AI says. JusPrin already works this way in other places: the "Printer added" card and the box where you type the access code are drawn by the app, not written by the AI.
2. Let the app pick fixed text. The Connect… opening line is already written by the app, not the AI (`printerWords.ts`). It just needs to check whether the plug-in is there and pick different wording. That fixes the wrong first question with no AI involved.
3. Where you do write rules, write general ones. One rule like "when something can't be done, pass on the reason the app gives" covers this case and many future ones. A rule about the plug-in specifically covers only this one.
4. Catch slips with tests, not more rules. Run realistic conversations and check the results, like the test I added. When something slips, fix it with 1 or 2 where possible, and add a rule only as a last resort.

So for this case I'd do 1 and 2: the app shows the plug-in notice and chooses the right opening line. The AI then only has to be helpful around it, and it no longer matters if it words things vaguely.
