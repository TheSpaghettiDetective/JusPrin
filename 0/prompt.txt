Pricing research results from the forked side session, plus Kenneth's instruction: do not jump to conclusions yet; at this step draw patterns, ask questions, and above all ask what additional information would let us make the optimal decision.

Kenneth asked the fork whether other AI-agent SaaS businesses had already solved the pricing problem we are brainstorming. Six web searches (2026 sources, mostly vendor blogs and third-party trackers, so hold exact figures loosely). What they show:

1. Hybrid pricing (flat subscription + metered usage unit) is the norm for AI agent products: Orb's 2026 study of 80 AI agent companies reports 95% hybrid; a May 2026 B2B survey found 37% hybrid and 29% on a credit/token unit. Credits are a packaging of usage inside a plan, not a separate model. (withorb.com/blog/2026-state-of-ai-agent-pricing-models-trends-and-whats-working; getmonetizely.com 2026 guide)
2. The main cautionary tale is Cursor, June 2025: Pro went from 500 counted "fast requests" to a $20 dollar-value token pool. People ran out after a handful of expensive-model prompts, got surprise overage charges because spend limits were off by default, and felt misled about staying on the old plan. CEO apology and refunds in July 2025. The unit the person cannot predict (dollars of tokens) felt like a trap; a countable unit (requests/chats) did not; overage must be opt-in; and changing the unit after launch is far more painful than picking it at launch. GitHub Copilot went the opposite way (premium requests -> 1,500 cent-denominated credits), so the industry has not settled which unit wins. (wearefounders.uk Cursor timeline; fintechweekly.com; vantage.sh/blog/cursor-pricing-explained; aiplanfinder.com/changelog)
3. The big consumer products avoid monthly caps: Claude Pro uses a rolling 5-hour window, ChatGPT Plus a 3-hour window, Perplexity Pro a daily allowance. Rolling windows cap burst cost without the "I used up my month" feeling. (aiplanfinder.com/ai-usage-limits-comparison)
4. A real sub-$10 consumer band exists: Google AI Plus $4.99, ChatGPT Go $8, consumer guides putting the entry tier at ~$9. These are positioned as "more than free, below the $20 tier", not as "the lesser model". (aizolo.com cheapest-ai-subscription; freemius.com AI app pricing)
5. No major AI product was found paying people credits or discounts for rating responses. OpenAI's community has several such requests and OpenAI replied it has no program; a Z.ai plugin issue asks for the same. Live programs (JumpTask etc.) pay cash for rating work as a separate business. A feedback-for-credits scheme would be novel: no playbook, and no evidence per-rating rewards yield useful data rather than click spam. (community.openai.com threads 1408785 and 1390155; github.com/zai-org/zcode-plugins/issues/68)
6. Gaps: nothing found on hobbyist 3D-printing software subscription pricing beyond Simplify3D's old $149 one-off and Shapr3D forum threads asking for a $12-15 hobby tier; nothing on the specific pattern of silently falling back to a cheaper model when a budget is exhausted.
