## 6. Verdict

These journeys do not show a prompt that is too complicated. Cutting it to a tenth lost five behaviours the product wants and fixed nothing; cutting the repetition lost nothing and fixed nothing. The cause of the failures here is that the text points at tools whose parameters the model cannot see.

Wording I would keep: the `once` text. It is a safe replacement, a quarter shorter, with one statement per rule.

- **Shown to be needed:** the first sentence of Intent, The object, "ask about consequential unknowns", the `presets_list` clause, and the first sentence of Verification.
- **Shown not to be needed on these journeys:** the role sentence, first person, "call the matching tool", every repeat, and the assess, read-settings, keep and recommend sentences of Setup (the model did all four unasked).

## 7. Cost and caveats

`bare` used 668,461 input and 39,102 output tokens; `once` used 805,274 and 43,755. The total is 1,473,735 input and 82,857 output, about **$0.15** at list price.

- **The instructions are a small part of a request.** A first request is 4,214 tokens with the control and 3,630 with `bare`. `bare`'s lower total comes from doing less, not from being shorter.
- **Three runs, one batch.** The control's own spread on `desk-ornament` is as large as the `once` difference.
- **`outdoor-fitting` cannot separate the arms** on its PETG and sliced rows, because it hits the refusal limit in every arm.
- **Untested, not shown unneeded:** export, Send, object overrides, "do not repeat an assessment", and plan recording (no tracked outcome depends on a plan).
- **Spool-versus-preset rule:** control and `once` asked what is loaded in 3 of 6 `outdoor-fitting` runs, `bare` in 0 of 3. That is too few to call.
- **`once` edits many sentences at once,** so a loss could not have been pinned on one of them. None appeared.

**Helped most: loading `slice_report` with the ordered text.**
